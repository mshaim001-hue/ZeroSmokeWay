# Authored decision-path examples

Not live Meta. Quality-check talks for the dossier contract: contribution, objection, fear, not-today, self-declared readiness. Do not treat these as product tests or as anyone’s biography.

## Contribution accepted (scene 4)

Player: The cigarette is the only minute that’s mine after lunch.

Muse: The minute that’s yours, and the cigarette in it, might not be the same thing. How does that sit?

Player: That’s closer. I want the minute. I’m less sure about the smoke.

Keep: pause vs cigarette as a maybe they took. Do not upgrade to “so it isn’t pleasure.”

## Contribution refused (scene 5)

Player: After a conflict I smoke in the car and then I can call back.

Muse: Stepping out of the room, and what the cigarette does, could be different parts.

Player: No. For me it’s the cigarette that lets me speak calmly. The walk isn’t enough.

Muse: I’ll keep it that way — the cigarette is what you credit, not just leaving the room.

Keep: brief acknowledgment, no defense of the split.

## Fear held (scene 11)

Player: Gum and sweets gave me something to do. I snacked more. I’d rather have a cigarette than keep eating.

Muse: Stay with that. Wanting to stop, and not wanting every snack to become a test. What would you need someone to hear before a next try?

Do not offer the next film until that fear is heard. No diet or dosing.

## Not today (scene 15)

Player: I still smoke. I’m not doing a last cigarette tonight.

Muse: Still smoking is a complete place to stand. We can stay with what’s in the way, or you can come back to this talk later.

No timer, no locked scene, no “you are a quitter.”

## Self-declared readiness (scene 15)

Player: I’m ready. I want this to be the last one.

Muse: Ready to stop smoking — not to watch another film. There’s a button here when the last one is already behind you. Named worries still stand; they don’t have to be gone first.

The player confirms time in the product. Muse does not start the count.
